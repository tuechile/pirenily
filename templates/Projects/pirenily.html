Emily is my English name. I picked it in sixth grade, my brother agreed, and then nobody ever used it. I tried again in college and everyone said I don’t sound like an Emily. It stayed mine anyway.

Iron Pig was my fan fiction author name: I loved Iron Man, and my middle school classmates called me a pig because I eat too fast.

Emily + Iron Pig = Pirenily.

{% endcall %} {% call cs.section("The Finder", lead="Every name I’ve been called") %}

The search bar on the landing page flashes through names: ones people call me, ones I call myself, and ones nobody calls me at all. Chi Le, Emily, Iron Pig, Chi Chi Cha Cha, Le Le, and:

— Emily Tiles: Chi Le, but the Ch is a T. Also, I like tile textures
— Chi Bell: what people call me a lot in Vietnam
— Kai: how some people read my name. I’m not Greek
— The Country: yes, like Chile

Type anything into it and it finds the closest page, forgiving typos, accents and half-typed words. If nothing matches, it opens a random page.

{% endcall %} {{ cs.placeholder("Screen recording: the names flashing, then typing into the finder") }} {% call cs.section("Why an Archive", lead="A niche, weird website") %}

Pirenily was my CS50 final project. I didn’t want to build something I’d never use again, and the idea of an archive has always fascinated me, so I built a place to keep everything.

I’ve always been a designer, but I didn’t know code before the US. In Vietnam, “IT” class meant Microsoft Office certification tests. Finding out code could be used for art made AFVS 55P my favorite class, and the best first class I could have asked for.

By the end of that semester I had a pile of interactive projects. So: I want to be a niche artist, let’s build a niche, weird website.

I’m a maximalist, but I don’t want to be maximalist about one thing. I want to be everything at once. So the frame stays minimal, and the work gets to be everything.

{% endcall %} {% call cs.section("First Website", lead="December 8, 2024") %}

My first ever website, built in one sitting: 32 commits between 3:37 in the afternoon and 10:57 at night, starting from a Flask template.

AI was bad at design back then, which was perfect. It couldn’t make a single design decision for me, so I planned everything myself and made ChatGPT build exactly what I described.

Most of what defines the site was already there that night: the names in the finder, the loader GIF between pages, BHN Cinema for the logo, Crimson Text for everything else. It hasn’t changed that much, because it still says what I want it to say.

Ideas show up all day: in class, looking at a poster, anywhere. I don’t write them down. Writing cleans out the stuttering and the fast talking, and then I can’t remember what I meant.

So I talk. Voice-to-text, and voice notes to my brother. When he replies, I have to defend the idea, and I can feel what I felt when I said it.

Building happens later, when my brain is off. The thinking is already done, so I can hyperfixate and just make it.

{% endcall %} {% call cs.row("pair", caption="My view and Your view, side by side.") %} {{ cs.placeholder("My view (dark, serif)") }} {{ cs.placeholder("Your view (light, sans)") }} {% endcall %} {% call cs.section("Two Views", lead="One for me, one for you") %}

My view (dark) is the site for me. It grew out of artists I love, especially people from the School for Poetic Computation, and Chia Amisola most of all: minimal, but still a little overstimulating. Her old website is the most beautiful thing I’ve seen on the internet.

Your view (light) is the accessible one, for recruiters or anyone who isn’t comfortable reading in the dark. It borrows from industry UX: larger type, a plain sans font, a warm paper background.

The nav has two switches, ☀ light and ♪ sound, instead of a settings panel. Every color comes from a small set of tokens, so one switch repaints the whole site, icons included.

Andy Dieu, my site’s biggest fan (he advertises it more than I do), told me it was starting to look vibe coded. He was right: I’d let Claude make too many design decisions for me.

So I stepped back. Pinterest, my old art, websites I used to love. I asked what each template was doing, what I could cut, and what would make it look like me again.

Then I wrote my decisions down as a token library, Pirenily UI, so the next time I doubt one, I can see why I made it and change it on purpose.

{% endcall %} {{ cs.placeholder("Pirenily UI: the token library") }} {% call cs.section("Drawn by Hand", lead="Every icon is a line drawing") %}

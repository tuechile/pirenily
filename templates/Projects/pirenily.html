How I Built This Website

{% if narration %} {% endif %}

Hey, sorry if I say “niche” or “chic” a lot. Those are my vocal stims right now, and as you’ll notice, this whole website is full of them.

One thing first: I don’t think AI restricts design. I think it makes it more accessible. I started this site with a taste, but no coding skills, and I kind of learned it off of ChatGPT when it was really bad. Now Claude writes most of the code (instead of me manually checking if moving 0.01rem is perfectly aligned), organizes feedback, and tracks progress, and I spend my time on the part that’s mine. You can see it in my commit messages from 2024, how I was struggling, manually checking if moving 0.01rem is perfectly aligned.

Pirenily is two of my names put together. Emily is my English name. I picked it in sixth grade (Chi Le Emily sounds so chic, hello??), my brother approved, and then nobody ever called me that. In college I tried to finally be an Emily, and everyone told me I don’t look or sound like one. So nobody uses it, but yeah, it’s been mine since sixth grade.

Iron Pig was my author name back when I wrote fan fiction. I was a huge Iron Man fan, and my middle school classmates called me a pig because I eat too fast (non-offensively, I did NOT get bullied). Emily + Iron Pig = Pirenily. Yeah, unfortunately my name is not “Piren, I love you” (the fact that at least three people asked this is crazy).

The search bar on the landing page flashes through more of my names: ones people call me, ones I call myself, and ones nobody calls me at all. Chi Chi Cha Cha and Le Le are joke names my friends call me. Emily Tiles, because Tiles is Chi Le with the Ch turned into a T, and I love a tile texture. Chi Bell, which is what people call me in Vietnam. Kai, because that’s how some people read my name (I’m not Greek). And The Country, as in Chile.

I’ve always been a self-taught designer, but I didn’t know code before I came to the US. Vietnam doesn’t really teach it; “IT” class meant studying for Microsoft Office tests. So finding out that code could be used for art was so much fun, and it’s why AFVS 55P is my favorite class, and the best first class I could have asked for.

By the end of that semester I had a pile of interactive projects, and a CS50 final project to make. I didn’t want to build a final project I’d never use again, and archives have always fascinated me. So I thought: I want to be a niche artist. Let’s build a niche, weird website, and keep everything there.

I’m a big maximalist, but I don’t want to be maximalist about just one thing. I want to be everything at once. So the site stays minimal, and everything inside it gets to be loud.

That’s the one idea every decision goes through: an archive. Quiet, so the work is the loudest thing on the page. Personal, so it’s mine and not a template. A little weird, because a niche artist gets a niche website. If something doesn’t fit, it goes, even if I love it.

— Black, with gray text. The frame should disappear, so the work is the brightest thing you see.
— One color, teal. If there’s only one, it means something when it shows up.
— Serif everywhere, one display font for the name. It should read like a book, not an app. Pirenily gets its own font because it’s the title.
— Blur on hover, not a color change. Things go soft when you look at them, and the palette stays quiet.
— No fades. Things just appear and disappear, like flipping through a box of photos.
— No rounded corners, and no stretched-out letters. Rounded buttons look like an app. Wide letter spacing is cringe.

I built the first version in December 2024, over 40 hours straight with no sleep and five teas. Caffeine doesn’t work on me. AI was still really bad at design then (not that it’s THAT good now, it has just improved a lot), and honestly that was perfect timing. It couldn’t make a single design decision that satisfied me, so I planned everything myself and pushed ChatGPT to build exactly what I had in mind. It looks kind of basic now, but not that basic, and there’s a lot going on. It’s still up, next to my high school site, which was already called Pirenily:

Not much has changed since, because it still says what I want it to say. Even the loader is the same one.

A few things people ask about. The search bar doesn’t need you to spell anything right. It tries, in order: the exact name, a name hiding inside what you typed, the start of a name you’re still typing, and then the closest spelling. Try it here, it won’t take you anywhere:

It’ll tell you where you’d land.

Hovering doesn’t change colors; things just go soft. Hover over this. In the light view it dims instead.

The ☀ and ♪ in the nav are the only settings. ☀ swaps the whole site between the two views, and ♪ turns on music and click sounds. Everything is colored from a few shared values, so one switch repaints every page, icons included.

My ideas come all day. In class, when I look at a poster, whenever. I don’t write them down, because writing cleans out the stuttering and the fast talking, and later I can’t tell what I was feeling. So I talk instead: voice-to-text, and voice notes to my brother (thanks, I love him a lot). When he answers, I have to defend the idea, and I remember exactly what I felt when I said it. You can turn my voice on and off and see how different the same idea can sound to you.

At night my brain turns off, and that’s when I build. The ideas are already there, so I don’t have to think. I just hyperfixate and make them.

The site has two views, and the ☀ in the nav switches between them. The dark one is for me. It started from artists I love: people from the School for Poetic Computation, Alicia Nguyen, Wyatt Roy’s 2024 site, Baku89, and most of all Chia Amisola. Her work is minimal but still a little overstimulating, and her old website is the most beautiful thing I’ve ever seen.

Andy Dieu is this site’s biggest fan (sorry Andy, I gotta glaze myself somehow); he’s advertised it more than I have (but this is true). At one point he told me it was starting to look vibe coded, and he was right. I’d been letting Claude make design decisions for me. This was truly devastating, because I kind of already knew, but I kept being in denial because I didn’t think it looked that vibe coded. Talking to Andy made me realize that even minimalism like this can define my artistic statement, and how much my trajectory changed.

So I stepped back. I went through my Pinterest, my old art and websites I used to love, and asked what each template was doing and what would make it feel like me again. Then I wrote my decisions down in Pirenily UI, so if I ever doubt one, I can see why I made it and change it on purpose.

The code is Flask and plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and most of it was written by Claude, from my decisions.

If you’re making your own: talk your ideas out. Decide before AI can decide for you. Write down why you made every choice, and why you said no to the others, because you won’t remember, no matter how sure you are right now. And just sit down and do it. Even if you don’t have an idea yet, sit down and start, and the ideas come.

I don’t want this to be a story about who I am. Mostly I hope you look around and think: oh okay, this is kinda cute. Hate my website? Pull an issue at github.com/tuechile/pirenily.

CommonSpirit Health is the largest nonprofit health system in the U.S., with 138 hospitals in 24 states. Its Community Investment Program (CIP) lends money, mostly below market rate, to groups working on the social causes of poor health in those communities. To decide where it goes, staff read each hospital's Community Health Needs Assessment (CHNA).

A T4SG team built an analysis tool for this the year before. It worked, but staff still couldn't compare a health priority across hospitals, regions or years, and the color-heavy, text-heavy dashboard felt "overwhelming". So they kept going back to the PDFs.

How might CIP staff compare health priorities across 200+ communities without reading every report?

{% endcall %} {% call cs.section("Research", label="small") %}

I interviewed 6 CommonSpirit board members one on one.

Feature decisions by impact × effort

{{ cs.card(img('ux', 'commonspirit/impact-effort.png'), "Impact x Effort prioritization board mapping feature ideas from research", "sm") }}

We ranked every idea from research (KPI numbers, region maps, hospital comparison charts, batch upload, notes and more) by impact and effort. That decided what came first and what we left out on purpose.

{% endcall %} {{ cs.boxes([ ("Home", "Holds the numbers staff check first."), ("Regional", "Many priorities turned out to be shared well beyond one city."), ("Compare", "The CIP team's original ask."), ("Trends", "Kept the one part of the old tool staff praised most."), ], title="Four pages made the cut") }} {% call cs.section() %}

Four pages plus settings didn't fit in the old top nav, so we moved it to a left sidebar. We also swapped the generic dashboard theme for CommonSpirit's own colors, so the tool looked like theirs.

Where the nav lives, how many clicks it takes to reach a comparison, whether the colors feel like theirs: these mattered as much as any single screen. Using CommonSpirit's own palette made the tool feel like it belonged to them, not to an outside team.

Status: shipped. I wrote the handoff document, and a T4SG engineering team built on it over the summer (backend and a Supabase trial). I wasn't on that build.

What we'd track after launch: how long it takes to compare one priority across hospitals, against reading the PDFs by hand.
